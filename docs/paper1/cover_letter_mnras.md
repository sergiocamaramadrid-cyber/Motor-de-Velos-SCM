# Cover Letter — MNRAS submission
## Recommended version (sober / classical tone)

---

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, entitled

"Environmental modulation of outer disk dynamics in nearby galaxies: evidence from SPARC"

for publication in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society.

This paper presents the SCM (Shear-Corrected MOND) framework, in which a local
observable, F3, is introduced as a phenomenological correction term in the analysis
of galaxy rotation curves. Using the SPARC sample, we show that this term improves
predictive performance relative to a baseline relation under repeated out-of-sample
validation.

Across repeated 70/30 galaxy-level train-test splits, the SCM model improves
out-of-sample performance in all test cases considered within the current validation
setup, with a median RMSE improvement of approximately −7.3 in proxy units and a
one-sided Wilcoxon p-value of 1.19 × 10⁻¹⁰. The manuscript is careful to interpret
these findings as empirical and phenomenological, rather than as a definitive
statement about underlying microphysics.

We believe the manuscript is suitable for MNRAS because it combines reproducible
statistical validation with a clearly defined observational framework relevant to
galaxy kinematics and scaling-law phenomenology.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration for publication elsewhere.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

## Extended version (more context, slightly promotional)

Dear Editors,

Please find enclosed our manuscript entitled:

"Environmental modulation of outer disk dynamics in nearby galaxies: evidence from SPARC"

for consideration as an original research article in Monthly Notices of the Royal
Astronomical Society.

In this work, we present the SCM (Shear-Corrected MOND) framework, a phenomenological
extension of standard baryonic scaling relations in galaxy rotation-curve analysis.
The central result is that a local correction term, denoted F3, carries additional
predictive information beyond the baseline relation when applied to the SPARC galaxy
sample.

Using repeated 70/30 galaxy-level train-test splits, we find that the SCM model
systematically improves out-of-sample performance relative to the baseline model.
In the current validation setup, the SCM term improves predictive accuracy in all
test cases considered, with a median out-of-sample RMSE improvement of approximately
−7.3 in proxy units, and a one-sided Wilcoxon test yielding p = 1.19 × 10⁻¹⁰.
We interpret these results conservatively as evidence for the empirical relevance
of the F3 term, without making a definitive claim regarding its underlying
microphysical origin.

We believe this manuscript will be of interest to readers of MNRAS working on galaxy
dynamics, phenomenological tests of gravity, empirical scaling relations, and the
interplay between baryonic structure and outer-disk kinematics.

This submission is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors
have approved its submission. The analysis pipeline is fully reproducible, and the
manuscript clearly distinguishes empirical results from forward-looking physical
interpretation.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,

Sergio Cámara Madrid  
Independent Researcher  
Andalusia, Spain
