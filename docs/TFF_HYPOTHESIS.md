# Trapped Field Flux (TFF) Hypothesis

**Status:** Interpretative hypothesis (not part of core SCM results)

---

## Empirical basis

Across independent datasets:

- LITTLE THINGS → no detectable signal (ρ ≈ 0)
- SPARC (global) → weak modulation
- YANG (high mass) → significant correlation (ρ ≈ −0.46)
- MOJAVE → strong coupling (ρ ≈ 0.5)

This suggests a systematic increase of environment–observable coupling with mass.

---

## Hypothesis

Above a critical mass threshold (log M ≳ 9.5–10), astrophysical systems may retain large-scale magnetic flux against dissipation. External pressure (ram pressure, ambient density, turbulence) can compress this flux, increasing energy density and modifying observable properties.

Below this threshold, the flux is not retained, and no environmental coupling is observed.

---

## Predicted consequences

- Enhanced angular momentum transport in massive galaxies
- Increased jet collimation efficiency
- Reduction of structural scale in high-energy systems

---

## Mathematical sketch (heuristic)

E_avail ~ Φ_B^2 / R  
Φ_B ~ GM / (R β(M))

with β(M) encoding a transition near M_crit.

---

## Null hypothesis

Observed correlations are due to selection effects or systematic biases.

---

## Current evidence

- MOJAVE: p < 1e-4
- YANG (high mass): p ~ 1e-4
- LITTLE THINGS: null detection (control sample)

---

## Interpretation

TFF provides a possible physical explanation for the observed transition between:

- regime-dependent systems (galaxies)
- continuous coupling systems (jets)

---

## Note

This hypothesis is not required to reproduce SCM results and should be tested independently.
